# The business case: why StudyRoute can matter outside a competition

StudyRoute is a sandbox: no real money moves and no real bank, partner or user is involved. This page says
what a real version could be worth, using **only figures that have a source or that are plain arithmetic on
one**. Everything about revenue is a hypothesis to test, not a forecast. Nothing here is investment advice.

## 1. The corridor in numbers (sourced)

| Fact | Figure | Source |
|------|--------|--------|
| Money sent through Bangladeshi banks for overseas education, fiscal year 2025-26 | **USD 881.7 million**, a record: up 32.4% on 2024-25 (USD 665.9m) and up about 105% on 2021-22 (USD 430.2m) | Bangladesh Bank data as reported by The Observer, "Overseas Education Boom Drives Record Foreign Currency Outflow", 24 July 2026 (https://observerbd.com/news/584772) |
| Average total cost of sending USD 200 worldwide | **6.36%** in Q3 2025 (6.49% in Q1 2025); the UN Sustainable Development Goal target is 3% by 2030 | World Bank, Remittance Prices Worldwide (https://remittanceprices.worldbank.org). **These are small person-to-person remittances, not tuition**, so they show how costly the corridor type is in general, not what a tuition payment costs |
| Bangladeshi students studying abroad | about **53,000** in 2021 (UNESCO figure) | Reported by the British Council and others; sources differ on the year and on whether non-tertiary students count. Treat as an order of magnitude |
| Banks and student files | During the 2022-23 dollar shortage many banks stopped opening the "student file" an education payment needs | Press reports (The Daily Star, "Banks no longer opening student files"; UNB). Read as context for the paperwork pain, not as a measurement |

## 2. The value, with its sums

* **For families.** Each percentage point of cost taken off the USD 881.7m a year is **USD 8.8 million** a
  year (881.7 x 1%). That is our arithmetic, **not a forecast**: it assumes the whole volume could be priced
  as the engine compares it, and the engine's own providers, fees and spreads are invented.
* **In our worked example** (synthetic rates): for GBP 10,000 the cheapest provider costs 1.58% and the
  dearest 2.16%, a gap of BDT 9,200 on one payment. It shows how a comparison can matter; it is **not a real
  quote** and has not been compared with a real one (docs/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md, section C).
* **For banks.** A payment arrives with its documents checked, a risk score and a plain-language brief, so a
  reviewer can say yes or no faster. **We have not measured any time saved**; a pilot would.
* **For universities.** Fewer payments that arrive short or cannot be matched to an invoice.

## 3. Who could pay (hypotheses)

StudyRoute does not move money and cannot: in Bangladesh only authorised dealer (AD) banks may, and the
Bangladesh Bank circular of 30 September 2026 (section 10) has the bank release the amount in the
institution's estimate, from one branch. So the realistic customer is the **bank**, with the family as the
user.

| Idea | How it would earn | Sum (assumptions in the last column) |
|------|-------------------|---------------------------------------|
| Service fee from a partner bank per assisted payment | a small percentage of the volume handled | at 0.2% of the volume: **USD 17,600** a year at a 1% share of the USD 881.7m, **USD 88,200** at 5%, **USD 176,300** at 10% (881.7m x share x 0.2%) |
| Subscription from university international offices | a yearly fee per university for payment tracking and reports | **not sized**: we have no data to size it honestly |

These are modest on purpose. We would rather show small, checkable numbers than a large invented market.
The family savings are larger than the revenue, which is what a service that sits in front of a bank has to
accept; the upside is adjacent services (living-cost plans, the escrow for rent, compliance reports).

## 4. What would have to be true

1. A partner AD bank takes a pilot. **None exists.**
2. Real quotes confirm that the comparison saves money on real payments. **Not checked.**
3. Real parents and students find the one-sentence plan easier than their current process. **No real user
   has tried it.**
4. The rules are verified with the bank. **Four study-abroad rules are anchored to the official circular; the
   rest are placeholders** (docs/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md, section B).

The cheapest way to test 1 to 3 is a pilot with one bank branch and a few dozen families, comparing the
engine's ranking with the bank's own quote and timing the review desk against the usual process.
